## Oakmont — The Districts

### The Shape of the Town

Oakmont is organized the way its founders planned it: by purpose.

The heights and the water are kept for living. The centre is kept for commerce and the civic business of the town. The south works. The academics cluster on the eastern rise and out toward the older quarters. Where a district begins and ends was decided decades ago, and the roads still follow those decisions exactly.

- **Residential:** Northwood Heights, Greater Eastwood, New Eastwood, Riverside
- **Commercial & civic:** Downtown, Old Oakmont
- **Working:** Southbrook District
- **Academic:** Kingsridge, Education District, Westbridge, Blackridge
- **Recreational:** Grand Lakes District

### The Residential Districts

**Northwood Heights.** The highest ground in Oakmont. Wooded, gated, and silent. Estates sit far back from private roads, screened by mature trees that were left standing on purpose. The roads up to the heights are swept daily; the gates rarely open, and never for the curious. Three of the town's oldest houses keep their principal residences here.

- *Character:* High-end residential zone, wooded heights, gated estates
- *Residents:* Victoria Beaufort, Jade Montclair, Isabella Laurent

**Greater Eastwood.** The old estate country to the east. Stone and timber, long lawns, driveways that curve so the house cannot be seen from the road. Greater Eastwood does not advertise what it costs. It was expensive before expensive was a category.

- *Character:* Traditional elite estates
- *Residents:* Ryan Carter, Marcus Conway

**New Eastwood.** The outlier. Glass and steel towers rose here within a single generation, catching lake light in the late afternoon like a second, shakier skyline. Newly developed elite housing — sleek, vertical, and still not entirely forgiven by the rest of the town. Locals have opinions about New Eastwood. They rarely say them out loud.

- *Character:* Modern luxury skyscraper residences, newly developed

**Riverside.** Along the river road, between the old poles and the new. Dark wood, clean lines, low lighting. Modern but restrained — architecture designed to be lived in rather than looked at. The water keeps the district unhurried.

- *Character:* Affluent residential district
- *Residents:* Madison Sterling, Ethan Parker, Tyler Knox

### The Town Centre

**Downtown.** The one part of Oakmont that belongs to everyone. Iron fronts and awnings along Maple Row, bloom in the window boxes come spring, strings of small lights over the square in winter. The shops are small and intentional. The morning queue at the café has not been a queue in years — it has been a conversation.

- *Character:* Primary commercial hub, major shopping
- *Locations:* Maple & Bean Café, Iron Lantern Pub

**Old Oakmont.** Stone and slate. The original town, kept deliberately small: government offices, the town hall and its clock tower, broad brick footpaths, brass fixtures gone green with age. Rossi's Italian Kitchen has held the same corner since before the second expansion, and the bells still mark the hour across the whole district.

- *Character:* Administrative and historic district
- *Locations:* Government offices, Rossi's Italian Kitchen, Town Hall & clock tower

### The Working South

**Southbrook District.** South of the brook that gave it its name. Brick, concrete, block glass, roll-up doors. Auto shops, yards, warehouses. The town pretends Southbrook is only industrial; Southbrook is where a great deal of what Oakmont actually needs gets built, delivered, and mended.

- *Character:* Industrial and service district
- *Locations:* Vitale Auto Garage

### The Academic Districts

**Kingsridge.** The eastern rise, and the highest-value ground outside the estates. Charlesworth sits here with its gates facing the town, and hotels line the approach for visiting families, fixtures, and ceremony. In match season, Kingsridge fills up and the whole district moves at the pace of the fixtures list.

- *Character:* Academic and high-value zone
- *Locations:* Charlesworth University, Hotels

**Education District.** A cluster of universities and schools sharing one quarter, one timetable season, and one long memory of results. Harrisfield is the anchor; several smaller institutions sit alongside it, unnamed but competitive. Rivalry runs through this district the way weather does — constant, seasonal, and impossible to opt out of.

- *Character:* Cluster of universities and schools
- *Locations:* Harrisfield, several smaller schools

**Westbridge.** Across the old bridge, west of the centre. Older, denser, louder. Westbridge University sits here among crowded streets, state-funded in origin and proud in a way Oakmont finds trying. Westbridge students fill the pubs downtown on Fridays and are walked home by the district's patience.

- *Character:* Older, denser academic quarter
- *Locations:* Westbridge University

**Blackridge.** The dark ridge on the town's northern edge. Working, hard, honest. Blackridge College turns out engineers, mechanics, and athletes instead of heirs, and its students carry themselves with the particular confidence of people who build things. Oakmont respects Blackridge in the grudging, specific way it reserves for places with something to prove.

- *Character:* Working district with a college tradition
- *Locations:* Blackridge College

### The Lakes

**Grand Lakes District.** The town's natural leisure ground — the largest of the three lakes gives the district its name. Cedar-shingled boathouses, floating docks, resorts with shallow stone terraces facing the water. In summer sailboats and white light; in winter bare grey docks, quietly beautiful. The pier opens the week the lake ice breaks.

- *Character:* Natural and leisure zone
- *Locations:* The lakes, Resorts, Recreational areas

### The Grounds

The town's fixtures play across five fixed venues, each tied to its district and its tenant:

- **Charlesworth Ground** — Kingsridge. Home ground of the Charlesworth Kings. Used for training and home matches.
- **Eastwood Stadium** — New Eastwood. Home ground of the Eastwood Lions.
- **Harrisfield Arena** — Education District. Home ground of the Harrisfield Falcons.
- **Southbrook Field** — Southbrook District. Smaller competitive venue.
- **Oakmont Centre** — near Grand Lakes District. Largest stadium in Oakmont. Primary venue for major matches, finals, and high-profile gatherings.
